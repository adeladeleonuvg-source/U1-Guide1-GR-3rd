Grammar 1

Before and After in Daily Routines

We use before and after to talk about the order of daily actions and routines. We can ask questions using "What do you do...?" or "What does he/she do...?" followed by before or after an activity (like school, breakfast, or lunch).

Structure for Questions

  • What + do/does + subject + do + before / after + noun?
  • Example: What do you do before school?
  • Example: What does she do after breakfast?

Structure for Answers

  • Subject + verb (present simple) + object.
  • Example: I brush my teeth.
  • Example: She feeds her fish. / He helps his mother.
Grammar 2

Adverbs of Frequency

Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens. In Unit 1, we focus on four key levels of frequency placed right before the main action verb:

always 100% of the time (Every single day)
usually 80% of the time (Most days)
sometimes 50% of the time (Occasionally)
never 0% of the time (Not at all)

Note on third-person singular (He/She): Remember to add an 's' or 'es' to the verb (e.g., "He always gets up early", "She never makes her bed late").

Resolved Examples (Step-by-Step)

Basic Level Before & After / Simple Frequency

1. Unscramble the question: washes / his / face / He (before school)

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Identify the subject: He
2. Identify the verb action: washes his face
3. Combine with the time marker: He washes his face before school.

2. Answer the question: What do you do before breakfast?

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Think of a morning routine action done prior to eating.
2. Formulate sentence using 'I': I brush my teeth before breakfast.

3. Complete with an adverb of frequency: I _____ eat breakfast at 7:00.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Select an appropriate adverb representing daily consistency.
2. Answer: always (I always eat breakfast at 7:00).

4. Formulate a question using 'after school': What do you do...?

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Structure: What + do + you + do + after school?
2. Result: What do you do after school?

5. Order the sentence: She / helps / usually / at / home

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Subject: She
2. Adverb of frequency (goes right before verb): usually
3. Verb phrase: helps at home
4. Result: She usually helps at home.

Intermediate Level Combined Routines & Third Person Singular

6. Unscramble the question: does / after / What / do / he / school

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Question word: What
2. Auxiliary: does + he + do
3. Time phrase: after school
4. Result: What does he do after school?

7. Correct the grammar error: He never make his bed.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Identify subject pronoun: 'He' requires third-person singular verb ending (-s).
2. Correct verb: makes
3. Result: He never makes his bed.

8. Complete the sentence based on schedule: She (80% frequency) has a snack at 3:45.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Match 80% with the corresponding adverb: usually.
2. Result: She usually has a snack at 3:45.

9. Formulate an answer for: What does she do before school? (feeds / hamster / her)

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Subject: She
2. Verb with third person 's': feeds
3. Object: her hamster
4. Result: She feeds her hamster.

10. Create a sentence describing a partner's morning routine with 'sometimes'.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Choose partner pronoun: He or She.
2. Insert adverb: sometimes.
3. Result: He sometimes takes a shower before breakfast.

Advanced Level Multi-clause Integration & Complex Time/Frequency mapping

11. Combine before/after and frequency: Write about getting up early and eating breakfast.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Integrate adverb: I always get up early.
2. Integrate time sequence: I eat breakfast after getting up.
3. Result: I always get up early and I eat breakfast after that.

12. Transform first-person to third-person statement: "I sometimes do my homework at 5:30." (for Sofia)

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Change subject: Sofia (or She)
2. Keep adverb: sometimes
3. Conjugate verb with third person: does (do -> does)
4. Result: Sofia sometimes does her homework at 5:30.

13. Formulate a complex routine paragraph: Mention two actions before school and one after school.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Before actions: I wash my face and brush my teeth before school.
2. After action: I help my mother after school.
3. Result: I wash my face and brush my teeth before school. I help my mother after school.

14. Unscramble full dialogue question: breakfast / What / do / after / she / does

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Question word: What
2. Auxiliary + Subject + Main verb: does she do
3. Time preposition + noun: after breakfast
4. Result: What does she do after breakfast?

15. Analyze frequency schedule: "He (0%) comes home at 8:00." Formulate negative frequency sentence.

Step-by-step resolution:
1. Identify 0% frequency: never.
2. Apply third person verb form: comes.
3. Result: He never comes home at 8:00.

Critical Thinking & Logical Reflection

Discuss these questions with your classmates or teacher to deepen your understanding of time management and grammar structures:

Question 1: Order matters

Why is it important to use before and after correctly when describing your morning routine to someone else?

Question 2: Frequency in daily habits

How do adverbs like always and never help us explain our personal habits better than just saying "I do it"?

Question 3: Subject-Verb Agreement

When talking about your friend (he or she), why does the verb change (e.g., brush becomes brushes)? Can you explain the rule?
